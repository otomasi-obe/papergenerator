================================================================================
TOPIC GUIDE — Forensic Science
================================================================================
FIELD: Natural Sciences / Law
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: APA
SUBDISCIPLINE: Forensic biology, forensic chemistry, toxicology, trace evidence, digital forensics, crime scene investigation

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  200–300 words, structured according to disciplinary norms. Include background, objectives, design, setting, participants or samples, intervention/exposure, primary outcomes, key numeric results with CI/p-value where applicable, and practical implication.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Define the problem, population, setting, and disciplinary significance.
  - Summarize burden, mechanism, current practice, and policy or clinical relevance.
  - Identify a precise evidence gap and justify why it matters.
  - State primary objective, secondary objectives, and hypothesis or research question.
  Subsections: Background, Rationale, Objectives.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  - Organize evidence from recent systematic reviews, guidelines, landmark trials, and high-quality observational studies.
  - Compare methods, settings, populations, measurement tools, and outcome definitions.
  - Identify inconsistent findings, under-studied subgroups, implementation barriers, and methodological limitations.
  - End with a synthesis that directly motivates the proposed study.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common designs: randomized trial, quasi-experimental study, cohort, case-control, cross-sectional survey, diagnostic accuracy study, mixed-methods study, implementation study, systematic review, and meta-analysis.
  Specify: setting, eligibility criteria, sampling strategy, sample size justification, variables, instruments, data collection procedures, quality control, bias mitigation, missing-data handling, and ethics approval.
  Quantitative analysis: descriptive statistics, effect sizes, 95% CI, regression modeling, survival analysis, diagnostic accuracy, subgroup/sensitivity analysis where appropriate.
  Qualitative analysis: purposive sampling, interview guide, coding framework, thematic analysis, trustworthiness, triangulation, reflexivity.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Report participant flow, baseline characteristics, primary and secondary outcomes, adverse events or unintended consequences.
  - Use absolute effects and clinically or practically meaningful interpretation, not only p-values.
  - Compare findings with prior literature and explain plausible mechanisms.
  - Discuss generalizability, limitations, residual bias, implementation implications, and future research.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Study flow diagram, conceptual framework, outcome trend chart, forest plot, subgroup plot, heat map, diagnostic curve, workflow diagram.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Baseline characteristics, measurement definitions, intervention components, primary outcomes, regression models, sensitivity analysis, evidence summary.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Directly answer the research objective.
  - State implications for practice, policy, education, or future research.
  - Avoid overclaiming beyond design, sample, and data limitations.

ADDITIONAL SECTIONS:
  - Ethical Approval / IRB statement.
  - Consent statement where human or animal participants are involved.
  - Data Availability Statement.
  - Funding and Conflicts of Interest.

DOMAIN VOCABULARY:
  Use field-specific terminology, validated instruments, standard outcome definitions, and accepted reporting guidelines such as CONSORT, STROBE, PRISMA, COREQ, CHEERS, ARRIVE, TRIPOD, or STARD when applicable.
