ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. Reports robotic task/environment, AI approach (perception/planning/RL), robot platform and sensors, simulation and real-world setup, key metrics (success rate, SPL, collision rate, sample efficiency, transfer gap), and implications for autonomy and deployment.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Robotics context: manipulation, navigation, field robotics, service robotics, human-robot collaboration, autonomous systems.
  - Core challenge: partial observability, uncertainty, sparse rewards, real-time constraints, safety-critical interaction, domain shift.
  - Research gap: limited generalization, low sample efficiency, brittle policies, weak sim-to-real transfer, inadequate robustness validation.
  - Objective: design, train, evaluate, or deploy AI-driven robot intelligence systems.
  - Contributions: model architecture, policy-learning strategy, perception-planning integration, transfer method, benchmark dataset, deployment insight.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Related Work" / "Background"
  - Robot intelligence paradigms: behavior trees, hybrid deliberative-reactive systems, learning-based control, world models.
  - Perception: RGB/RGB-D/LiDAR processing, 3D detection, semantic mapping, visual-language grounding for robotics.
  - Planning: sampling-based planners (RRT/RRT*), optimization-based MPC, task and motion planning (TAMP), hierarchical planning.
  - Reinforcement learning: DQN, PPO, SAC, TD3, model-based RL, offline RL, imitation learning, curriculum learning.
  - Sim-to-real: domain randomization, system identification, adaptation, residual learning, policy distillation.
  - Multi-robot AI: decentralized coordination, communication learning, cooperative planning, swarm intelligence.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Methodology" / "System and Learning Framework"
  - Task definition: observation space, action space, reward design, constraints, termination criteria.
  - Platforms and sensors: manipulator/mobile robot/legged robot/drone, camera, depth, LiDAR, IMU, force sensors.
  - Simulation setup: Isaac Gym, Gazebo, PyBullet, MuJoCo, CoppeliaSim; scene generation, physics parameters, disturbance models.
  - Model design: perception encoder, policy/value networks, planner interface, memory modules, uncertainty estimators.
  - Training strategy: RL/imitation/self-supervised learning, hyperparameters, replay strategy, domain randomization schedule.
  - Transfer protocol: sim-to-real calibration, fine-tuning, adaptation loops, safety guards, deployment pipeline.
  - Evaluation: success rate, path efficiency, collision count, policy stability, sample efficiency, transfer performance gap, robustness under perturbation.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Perception studies report detection/localization precision-recall, pose error, latency, robustness to lighting and occlusion.
  - Planning studies report planning time, trajectory smoothness, collision-free rate, replanning stability in dynamic scenes.
  - RL studies report episodic return, success convergence, sample complexity, policy robustness, and ablation insights.
  - Sim-to-real studies report transfer gap, real-world failure modes, adaptation gains, and safety-performance trade-offs.
  - System-level discussion emphasizes deployment constraints: onboard compute, sensor noise, actuator delays, calibration drift, and safety margins.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - System architecture, policy learning curves, reward progression, perception outputs, trajectory comparisons, sim-to-real pipeline diagrams, failure-case visualizations.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Benchmark comparison (method, task, success, sample efficiency), hyperparameters, ablation variants, transfer metrics, latency and compute footprint, robustness scenarios.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - States whether AI method improves autonomy, robustness, efficiency, or transferability on robotic tasks.
  - Clarifies trade-offs between performance, safety, compute cost, and data requirements.
  - Discusses limitations: domain mismatch, sparse real data, hardware constraints, reproducibility issues.
  - Suggests future work: foundation policies, multimodal reasoning, continual adaptation, safer exploration, large-scale real-world validation.
