FIELD: Agriculture / Engineering
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: IEEE or APA
SUBDISCIPLINE:
  Precision agriculture, agricultural IoT, UAV remote sensing, crop disease detection, irrigation control, robotics, decision support, livestock monitoring, farm data platforms, cybersecurity, and climate-smart agriculture.

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  Abstracts should identify the crop, livestock, farm system, or platform; state the sensing or modelling problem; describe dataset, hardware, algorithm, field design, and evaluation metrics; report quantitative accuracy, resource savings, yield effects, or adoption outcomes; and state deployment implications.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  Establish agronomic, environmental, productivity, labor, water, or resilience problem; describe field context and technology gap; define measurable variables and stakeholders; justify sensing, automation, analytics, or decision-support approach; end with objectives, research questions, or hypotheses.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Synthesize recent work on precision monitoring, sensor networks, UAV imagery, AI diagnosis, smart irrigation, robotics, yield forecasting, livestock analytics, data platforms, connectivity, and sustainability; compare datasets, algorithms, validation settings, limitations, and adoption barriers.

METHODOLOGY:
  precision farming, drone/UAV imagery, NDVI analysis, soil sensor network, crop disease detection using CNN, irrigation optimization, yield prediction, edge computing, GIS mapping, decision-support modelling, field trials, sensor calibration, model validation, geospatial analysis, and cost-benefit assessment.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  Report detection accuracy, prediction error, water savings, yield gains, energy use, latency, coverage, robustness across seasons, sensor reliability, farmer usability, economic feasibility, sustainability indicators, and comparison with baseline agronomic practice or conventional models.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  UAV field imagery, NDVI map, disease detection sample, sensor network diagram, irrigation schedule chart, yield prediction plot, dashboard mockup, architecture diagram, confusion matrix heatmap, geospatial prescription map, and robot path plan.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  crop yield by treatment, detection accuracy, sensor comparison, irrigation water use, model hyperparameters, farm cost-benefit analysis, sustainability indicators, dataset summary, hardware specifications, connectivity performance, and baseline comparison.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  State the validated smart-agriculture contribution, quantify agronomic or operational benefit, identify deployment constraints, discuss scalability for smallholder or commercial farms, and recommend seasonal validation, integration, or farmer-centered implementation.
