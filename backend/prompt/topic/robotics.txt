DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: IEEE
SUBDISCIPLINE: Robot Design, Kinematics, Dynamics, Motion Control, Manipulation, Mobile Robotics, Human-Robot Interaction, Robot Systems Engineering

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. Reports robot platform or subsystem, task environment, mechanical/control design, sensing and actuation setup, experimental or simulation protocol, performance metrics, and implications for reliability, accuracy, safety, or deployment.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Robotic application context: manufacturing, service, healthcare, agriculture, logistics, inspection, education, or field robotics.
  - Engineering problem: accuracy, stability, payload, workspace, energy efficiency, safety, robustness, cost, or maintainability.
  - Gap in existing systems: limited kinematic reach, poor trajectory tracking, fragile locomotion, insufficient sensing, weak validation.
  - Objective: design, model, control, simulate, or experimentally evaluate a robotic system or component.
  - Contributions: robot mechanism, controller, kinematic/dynamic model, trajectory planner, prototype, or benchmark evaluation.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Related Work" / "Background"
  - Robot kinematics: DH parameters, forward/inverse kinematics, Jacobian, singularity analysis.
  - Robot dynamics and control: Lagrange/Newton-Euler, PID, computed torque, adaptive control, impedance/admittance control.
  - Motion planning: configuration space, trajectory generation, RRT/RRT*, PRM, spline/polynomial planning.
  - Sensing and perception for robotics: encoders, IMU, force/torque, LiDAR, RGB-D, sensor fusion.
  - Robot platforms: manipulators, mobile robots, legged robots, aerial robots, soft robots, continuum robots.
  - Safety and standards: collaborative robot safety, risk assessment, fail-safe design, human-robot workspace constraints.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Methodology" / "System Design and Evaluation"
  - Mechanical design: CAD modeling, material selection, actuator sizing, payload and workspace analysis.
  - Modeling: forward/inverse kinematics, dynamic model, transfer function/state-space representation.
  - Control: PID, MPC, adaptive, robust, impedance, force control, trajectory tracking.
  - Simulation: MATLAB/Simulink, ROS/Gazebo, Webots, CoppeliaSim, MoveIt, finite element or multibody simulation.
  - Experiment: prototype setup, calibration, sensors, sampling rate, test trajectories, task scenarios.
  - Metrics: position error, orientation error, settling time, overshoot, path length, energy use, success rate, payload, repeatability.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Kinematic validation reports workspace, singularities, reachable poses, and IK convergence.
  - Control results compare tracking error, stability, settling time, and disturbance rejection.
  - Planning results report collision-free success, computation time, path smoothness, and clearance.
  - Prototype experiments discuss mechanical tolerance, sensor noise, actuator saturation, and safety constraints.
  - Simulation-to-prototype differences are explained through friction, backlash, compliance, and calibration limits.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Robot CAD model, kinematic diagram, DH frames, control block diagram, trajectory plots, workspace envelope, simulation screenshot, experimental setup.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - DH parameters, actuator specifications, sensor specifications, controller gains, trajectory tracking errors, payload tests, planner benchmark results.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - States whether the robot design/control approach met accuracy, stability, payload, safety, or efficiency goals.
  - Discusses deployment limitations: hardware cost, calibration, robustness, environment assumptions, safety validation.
  - Suggests future work: improved sensing, robust control, field trials, lightweight design, autonomous integration, standards compliance.
