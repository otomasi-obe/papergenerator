================================================================================
TOPIC GUIDE — Philosophy & Ethics
================================================================================
FIELD: Arts and Humanities / Philosophy
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: Chicago or MLA
SUBDISCIPLINE: Etika, Estetika, Epistemologi, Logika, Filsafat Politik, Filsafat Ilmu, Filsafat Agama

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. States: philosophical problem addressed, argumentative method,
  key philosophical position advanced, contribution to the debate.
  Example: "This paper defends a virtue-theoretic account of epistemic responsibility..."

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Statement of the philosophical problem or question.
  - Why the problem matters: real-world stakes or theoretical significance.
  - Map of philosophical positions in the debate.
  - Statement of the paper's thesis/position.
  - Outline of argument structure ("The paper proceeds as follows...").

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Background" / "The Debate" / "Prior Arguments"
  - Engage with canonical philosophical texts: Kant, Aristotle, Hume, Mill, Rawls, Habermas.
  - Survey contemporary philosophical debate in the specific area.
  - For ethics: deontology, consequentialism, virtue ethics, care ethics positions.
  - For epistemology: foundationalism, coherentism, reliabilism, contextualism.
  - For political philosophy: liberalism, communitarianism, republicanism.
  Key frameworks: Kantian ethics, Utilitarian calculus, Rawlsian veil of ignorance,
  Aristotelian virtue ethics, Frankfurt cases, thought experiments.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Argument" / "Philosophical Analysis" / "Method"
  - Conceptual analysis: clarifying meaning of key concepts.
  - Thought experiments: hypothetical cases to test intuitions.
  - Logical argument construction: premise–conclusion structure.
  - Modal logic (□, ◇) for necessity/possibility claims.
  - Reflective equilibrium: balancing principles with considered judgments.
  - Dialectical method: thesis, objection, response.
  - Genealogical analysis (Nietzsche/Foucault): tracing the history of concepts.
  No empirical data; uses rational argument, conceptual clarification, examples.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  Section name: "Argument" / "Defense" / "Analysis"
  - Philosophical argument presented with explicit premises and conclusion.
  - Reply to objections: "One might object that...; I respond by..."
  - Use of counterexamples and thought experiments to test positions.
  - Logical formalization where relevant: propositional/modal logic symbols.
  Tables: summary of philosophical positions and their commitments.
  Figures: argument map, logical relationship diagram.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Argument map/flowchart.
  - Decision tree (for ethical dilemmas).
  - Conceptual Venn diagram.
  - Modal logic formula display.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Comparison of ethical theories on a specific issue.
  - Summary of objections and replies.
  - Logical structure of argument (premise | claim | support).

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Restates the philosophical thesis and summarizes main arguments.
  - Acknowledges residual objections and their force.
  - Notes limitations: idealization, cultural assumptions, boundary conditions.
  - Suggests further philosophical inquiry.

ADDITIONAL SECTIONS:
  - "Thought Experiments": dedicated section presenting hypothetical scenarios.
  - "Formal Appendix": logical notation for complex arguments.
  - "Applied Implications": for applied ethics papers (bioethics, AI ethics, business ethics).
