TOPIC GUIDE — Operations Research

FIELD: Mathematics / Engineering / Business / Management Science
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: IEEE for algorithmic and engineering studies; APA for managerial decision science studies
SUBDISCIPLINE: Optimization, Simulation, Queuing Systems, Decision Science, Supply Chain Analytics, Scheduling, Network Models, Stochastic Operations

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. Reports operational decision context, mathematical or simulation model, data/scenario source, solution method, benchmark or baseline, key performance metrics, sensitivity analysis, and managerial implications.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Operational context: production, logistics, healthcare, transportation, energy, service systems, finance, or public operations.
  - Decision problem: cost reduction, capacity allocation, routing, scheduling, waiting time, risk, robustness, or service-level improvement.
  - Research gap: unrealistic assumptions, limited uncertainty modeling, weak scalability, lack of validation, or missing decision support.
  - Objective: formulate, solve, simulate, compare, or validate an operations research model.
  - Contributions: mathematical model, algorithm, simulation framework, decision-support tool, policy insight, or empirical benchmark.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Related Work" / "Background" / "Literature Review"
  - Optimization models: linear programming, integer programming, mixed-integer programming, nonlinear programming, robust optimization, stochastic programming.
  - Metaheuristics: genetic algorithms, simulated annealing, tabu search, particle swarm optimization, ant colony optimization, NSGA-II.
  - Simulation: Monte Carlo simulation, discrete-event simulation, agent-based simulation, system dynamics, digital twins.
  - Queuing theory: M/M/1, M/M/c, M/G/1, priority queues, finite capacity queues, Jackson networks.
  - Decision science: multi-criteria decision analysis, AHP, TOPSIS, PROMETHEE, utility theory, risk analysis.
  - Application domains: supply chains, inventory, scheduling, healthcare operations, transportation, energy, service systems.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Methodology" / "Model Formulation" / "Solution Approach"
  - Problem definition: decision variables, objective functions, constraints, assumptions, scope, and notation.
  - Data and scenarios: demand, processing time, arrival rate, service rate, costs, capacities, uncertainty distributions.
  - Optimization: LP/MILP/MINLP formulation, decomposition, branch-and-bound, column generation, dynamic programming, robust/stochastic programming.
  - Simulation: conceptual model, input distribution fitting, warm-up period, replications, random seed control, verification and validation.
  - Queuing analysis: arrival/service processes, utilization, waiting-time formulas, steady-state assumptions, capacity scenarios.
  - Decision analysis: criteria weighting, stakeholder preference elicitation, alternative ranking, consistency checks, sensitivity analysis.
  - Evaluation: optimality gap, runtime, cost, service level, throughput, waiting time, resource utilization, Pareto efficiency.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Optimization studies report objective value improvement, feasibility, runtime, optimality gap, and sensitivity to demand/cost/capacity.
  - Simulation studies report confidence intervals, bottlenecks, utilization, throughput, waiting time, and scenario comparison.
  - Queuing studies compare staffing/capacity policies using waiting time, queue length, utilization, and service-level targets.
  - Decision science studies report criteria weights, ranked alternatives, robustness of rankings, and stakeholder interpretation.
  - Managerial discussion translates model outputs into policy, staffing, routing, scheduling, investment, or risk mitigation decisions.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Optimization model flow, network diagram, Pareto frontier, convergence plot, sensitivity tornado chart, simulation process map, queueing system diagram, decision hierarchy.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Parameters and notation, decision variables, scenario definitions, optimal solutions, algorithm comparison, sensitivity results, queue performance metrics, MCDA ranking matrix.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - States whether the model improves cost, utilization, service level, waiting time, robustness, or decision quality.
  - Summarizes practical recommendations and boundary conditions for implementation.
  - Discusses limitations: assumptions, data quality, scalability, stochastic uncertainty, behavioral factors, or computational burden.
  - Suggests future work: richer uncertainty, real-time optimization, hybrid simulation-optimization, larger datasets, field deployment.
