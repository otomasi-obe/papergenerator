================================================================================
TOPIC GUIDE — History Studies
================================================================================
FIELD: Arts and Humanities / History
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: Chicago
SUBDISCIPLINE: Sejarah Kuno, Sejarah Medieval, Sejarah Modern, Sejarah Asia, Sejarah Amerika, Arkeologi

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–300 words. Elements: historical period, geographical focus, primary sources used,
  central historical argument/thesis, historiographical contribution.
  Example: "Drawing on previously unexplored colonial administrative records, this study argues..."

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Historical context of the period under study.
  - Identification of historiographical debate ("historians have long debated...").
  - Thesis statement presenting the paper's central historical argument.
  - Scope: time period, geography, social group studied.
  - Significance: why this history matters today.
  Subsections: Historical Background, Historiographical Overview, Thesis and Scope.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Historiography" / "Review of Scholarship" / "Literature Review"
  - Survey of existing historical scholarship on the topic.
  - Identify competing historiographical schools (Annales school, social history, cultural history).
  - Discuss seminal works by key historians (Braudel, Hobsbawm, Said, Zinn, Ranke).
  - Identify gaps in existing historical interpretation.
  Key frameworks: Social history, Cultural history, Postcolonial history, Gender history,
  Microhistory, Global/transnational history, History from below.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Sources and Methods" / "Archival Methodology" / "Research Method"
  Primary source analysis:
  - Archival research: government documents, administrative records, private correspondence.
  - Material culture analysis: artifacts, coins, architectural remains.
  - Oral history: interviews with witnesses/descendants.
  - Numismatic, epigraphic, paleographic analysis.
  Secondary source synthesis:
  - Historiographical analysis: comparing historical interpretations.
  - Comparative history: comparing events across regions/periods.
  - Quantitative history: demographic data, economic statistics.
  Archaeological methods (if applicable): stratigraphy, radiocarbon dating.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  Section name: "Analysis" / "Findings" / narrative chapters with subtitles
  - Chronological narrative supported by primary source evidence.
  - Argument that revises or confirms existing historiographical interpretation.
  - Detailed quotations from primary documents with analysis.
  - Discussion of causation, contingency, and historical actors' motivations.
  Estimated data: "analysis of 340 archival documents from 1820–1870",
  "examination of census data across 14 provinces".
  Tables: demographic statistics, chronological event tables.
  Figures: historical maps, photographs of primary sources, archaeological diagrams.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Historical maps showing territorial changes.
  - Photographs/facsimiles of primary documents.
  - Demographic/economic trend charts.
  - Archaeological site plans.
  - Timeline of key events.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Chronological event table (year | event | significance | source).
  - Comparative demographics across regions/periods.
  - Inventory of primary sources consulted.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Restates historical thesis with supporting evidence.
  - Positions contribution within historiographical debate.
  - Acknowledges limitations: fragmentary sources, regional focus.
  - Suggests future research directions (underexplored archives, comparative studies).
  - May include "Historical Significance" discussing contemporary relevance.

ADDITIONAL SECTIONS:
  - "Archival Sources": description of archives consulted.
  - "Note on Translation": methodology for translated primary sources.
  - "Glossary": key historical terms, place names, titles.
