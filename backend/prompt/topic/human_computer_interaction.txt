TOPIC GUIDE — Human Computer Interaction

FIELD: Computer Science / Design / Behavioral Science
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: APA for user studies and behavioral analysis; IEEE for interactive systems, devices, and engineering prototypes
SUBDISCIPLINE: Usability Engineering, UX Research, Interaction Design, CSCW, Accessibility, Ubiquitous Computing, Human-AI Interaction

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. Reports user population and context of use, interface or interaction intervention, study design, core usability/experience metrics, key behavioral insights, and practical implications for design guidelines or system improvement.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Human-centered context: tasks, environments, users, and stakes of interaction quality.
  - Problem framing: usability breakdown, cognitive overload, low adoption, exclusion, trust deficits, workflow friction.
  - Gap in literature/practice: underrepresented users, limited ecological validity, weak longitudinal evidence, narrow modality focus.
  - Research objectives: design, evaluate, compare, or model interactions across devices and contexts.
  - Contributions: novel interaction technique, validated instrument, design framework, empirical findings, or dataset/toolkit.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Related Work" / "Background"
  - Foundational HCI: user-centered design, activity theory, distributed cognition, affordances, Norman principles.
  - Usability frameworks: Nielsen heuristics, ISO 9241-11, cognitive walkthrough, GOMS, KLM.
  - UX and emotion: hedonic/pragmatic quality, flow, trust, engagement, frustration, fatigue.
  - Accessibility and inclusive design: WCAG, universal design, assistive technology integration.
  - Human-AI interaction: transparency, explainability, calibration of trust, automation bias.
  - Collaboration and social computing: CSCW, awareness cues, coordination costs, moderation and safety.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Method" / "Study Design"
  - Study types: controlled experiment, field study, diary study, lab usability test, longitudinal deployment, mixed methods.
  - Participants: recruitment criteria, sample diversity, power analysis, ethics and consent.
  - Data collection: task logs, clickstream, eye-tracking, think-aloud, interviews, surveys (SUS, UEQ, NASA-TLX, Trust scales).
  - Experimental design: within/between subjects, counterbalancing, A/B testing, factorial design.
  - Analysis: descriptive stats, t-test/ANOVA/MANOVA, regression/SEM, nonparametric tests, thematic coding.
  - Artifact prototyping: Figma/prototype tools, web/mobile implementation, sensor or tangible interaction setup.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Usability improvements are shown through reduced task time/errors and higher satisfaction scores.
  - Trade-offs between efficiency, learnability, and perceived control are discussed.
  - Accessibility outcomes emphasize diverse user performance and inclusion barriers.
  - Human-AI interaction findings focus on trust calibration, explanation utility, and overreliance risks.
  - Ecological validity, generalizability, and deployment constraints are critically addressed.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Interface screenshots, task flow diagrams, interaction timelines, eye-tracking heatmaps, workload/trust trend plots, prototype architecture.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Participant demographics, task definitions, usability metrics, questionnaire scores, statistical test summaries, coding themes.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - States whether interaction redesign improved effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction, and inclusion.
  - Highlights design principles derived from evidence and context limitations.
  - Recommends future studies with broader populations, longer deployments, and multimodal evaluation.
