TOPIC GUIDE — Library Science

FIELD: Social Sciences / Information Science
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: APA or Chicago
SUBDISCIPLINE: Information Organization, Digital Libraries, Bibliometrics, Archives, User Studies, Scholarly Communication

COMMON REFERENCE VENUES:
  Use leading journals, conferences, books, standards, and domain reports appropriate to the topic.

--- PAPER GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS ---
When given a user's research idea, you MUST generate a COMPLETE academic paper following these requirements:

1. RESEARCH GAP IDENTIFICATION:
   - Identify 3-5 specific gaps in current literature related to the user's topic
   - Reference recent works (2020-2025) that establish what is known
   - Clearly articulate what remains UNKNOWN or UNRESOLVED
   - Frame the gap as a researchable question with measurable outcomes
   - Ensure the gap targets Q1/Q2 Scopus-indexed journals

2. NOVEL TOPIC REFINEMENT:
   - Refine the user's idea into a publishable research title
   - The title must be specific, include method and application domain
   - Target journals listed in COMMON REFERENCE VENUES below

3. LATEST METHODOLOGY (2022-2025):
   - Use cutting-edge methods appropriate to the subdiscipline
   - Include complete research procedures and protocols
   - Specify all tools, software, instruments, and configurations
   - Detail sample size calculations, validation strategy, and reproducibility measures

4. ESTIMATED DATA & RESULTS:
   - Provide realistic estimated data tables (10-30 data points)
   - Include statistical measures: mean, std, 95% CI, effect sizes
   - Performance/outcome metrics appropriate to the field
   - Comparison with control groups or baseline conditions
   - All numbers must be realistic, internally consistent, and domain-appropriate
   - Data must support the research conclusions logically

5. MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS:
   - Include 5-10 relevant formulas with full variable definitions
   - Statistical tests, measurement models, analytical equations
   - Connect formulas to the methodology and results

6. LITERATURE REVIEW WITH EXPLICIT GAPS:
   - Minimum 25-30 references organized thematically
   - Structure: (a) foundational theories, (b) recent advances 2020-2025, (c) limitations
   - Each subsection MUST end with: "However, no study has addressed..."
   - Use synthesis paragraphs, not citation lists
   - Include Indonesian/Southeast Asian context where relevant

7. COMPLETE PAPER STRUCTURE:
   - Title, Abstract (200-250 words), Keywords (5-7)
   - Introduction (1500-2000 words)
   - Literature Review (2000-3000 words)
   - Methodology (2000-3000 words with formulas)
   - Results & Discussion (2000-3000 words with tables and figure descriptions)
   - Conclusion (500-800 words)
   - References (30+ entries, 60%+ from 2020-2025)
--- END PAPER GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS ---

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. State the library science problem, study context, data/source material,
  method, evaluation metrics, principal findings, contribution, limitation, and implication.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Define the empirical or conceptual problem and why it matters now.
  - Identify stakeholders, geographic/social scope, and practical consequences.
  - Position the work against current literature and policy/technical debates.
  - State research gap, research questions, objectives, and expected contribution.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Related Work" / "Background" / "Theoretical Framework"
  - Core theories, frameworks, standards, and domain concepts.
  - Recent empirical findings and methodological debates.
  - Dataset, corpus, archive, platform, or field-site precedents.
  - Limitations in existing evidence and opportunities for replication or extension.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Methods" / "Research Design"
  - Design: qualitative, quantitative, computational, experimental, comparative, or mixed methods.
  - Data: sampling frame, inclusion criteria, instruments, preprocessing, and ethics.
  - Analysis: model, coding scheme, validation strategy, robustness checks, and reproducibility.
  - Metrics: report validity, reliability, uncertainty, bias, and domain-specific performance indicators.

RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Present findings by research question, not by raw output sequence.
  - Compare outcomes with prior studies and explain contextual differences.
  - Discuss uncertainty, sensitivity, trade-offs, and alternative interpretations.
  - Tables: descriptive statistics, model results, comparative cases, coded themes.
  - Figures: conceptual framework, workflow, maps/networks/charts, result dashboards.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Study design workflow or analytical pipeline.
  - Conceptual framework or theory-of-change diagram.
  - Spatial, network, timeline, or distribution visualization as relevant.
  - Comparative result chart with confidence/uncertainty indicators.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Data/source summary: source | period | coverage | variables | limitations.
  - Method matrix: research question | data | method | metric | expected output.
  - Findings table: theme/model/case | evidence | interpretation | implication.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Answer the research questions directly and quantify or qualify the contribution.
  - State theoretical, methodological, and practical implications.
  - Acknowledge limitations in scope, data quality, generalizability, and bias.
  - Recommend future work with concrete datasets, sites, populations, or methods.

DOMAIN VOCABULARY:
  Information Organization, Digital Libraries, Bibliometrics, Archives, User Studies, Scholarly Communication

METHODOLOGY KEYWORDS:
  mixed-methods design, GIS/spatial analysis, survey research, case study comparison, statistical modeling, network analysis, content analysis, simulation modeling, interview-based inquiry, policy evaluation
