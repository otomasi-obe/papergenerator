TOPIC GUIDE — Information Systems

FIELD: Information Systems / Computer Science / Business
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: APA for socio-technical, organizational, and behavioral IS research; IEEE for architecture, implementation, and systems engineering studies
SUBDISCIPLINE: Enterprise Systems, Digital Transformation, IS Strategy, IT Governance, E-Government, Business Analytics, Design Science Research

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. Reports organizational context, IS problem, theoretical model or artifact, research method, key performance/adoption outcomes, and managerial or policy implications.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Organizational and digital context: industry pressures, data-driven operations, compliance, competitiveness.
  - Problem statement: misalignment between IT and business, low system adoption, process inefficiency, data silos, governance gaps.
  - Research gap: limited contextual evidence, weak theory-artifact integration, insufficient longitudinal evaluation.
  - Objective: explain, predict, design, or evaluate information systems and organizational outcomes.
  - Contributions: validated model, design artifact, governance framework, implementation evidence, or policy recommendations.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Related Work" / "Theoretical Background"
  - IS theories: TAM, UTAUT, DeLone-McLean IS Success, TOE, RBV, institutional theory, socio-technical systems.
  - IS strategy and governance: COBIT, ITIL, enterprise architecture, alignment maturity, digital capability.
  - Enterprise applications: ERP, CRM, SCM, HRIS, BI platforms, data warehouses, process mining.
  - Digital transformation: platformization, cloud migration, AI adoption, organizational change management.
  - Research paradigms: behavioral IS, design science research, action research, case research, mixed methods.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Methodology" / "Research Design"
  - Behavioral studies: survey design, construct validation, SEM/PLS-SEM, mediation/moderation analysis.
  - Design science: problem diagnosis, objective definition, artifact design, demonstration, evaluation, communication.
  - Case/action research: multi-site cases, interviews, document analysis, process tracing, intervention cycles.
  - Quantitative operations data: log analytics, process mining, KPI analysis, quasi-experimental evaluation.
  - Evaluation metrics: adoption intention/use, user satisfaction, net benefits, process efficiency, service quality, compliance.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Model-based studies report significant paths among constructs (e.g., perceived usefulness -> intention -> use).
  - Enterprise implementation studies emphasize process redesign, governance readiness, and change management.
  - Digital transformation outcomes include productivity, agility, resilience, and risk management trade-offs.
  - Design science papers discuss artifact utility, context fit, and transferability.
  - Limitations often involve single-sector samples, self-reported measures, and short observation windows.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Conceptual model diagrams, enterprise architecture views, process maps, dashboard mockups, adoption trends, governance framework diagrams.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Construct definitions and indicators, reliability/validity statistics, hypothesis testing, case summaries, KPI before/after comparisons.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Summarizes theoretical contribution and practical relevance for managers/policy makers.
  - Clarifies which factors and interventions drive IS success in context.
  - Recommends scaling strategy, governance refinement, and longitudinal validation.
