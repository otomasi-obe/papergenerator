3. LATEST METHODOLOGY (2022-2025):

   - Include complete research procedures and protocols

4. ESTIMATED DATA & RESULTS:
   - Provide realistic estimated data tables (10-30 data points)
   - Include statistical measures: mean, std, 95% CI, effect sizes
   - Performance/outcome metrics appropriate to the field
   - Comparison with control groups or baseline conditions

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–200 words. Identifies: literary work(s) analyzed, critical theory applied,
  argument about the text, broader cultural/social significance.
  Example: "Through a postcolonial lens, this paper examines how Pramoedya Ananta Toer's..."

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Introduction of the literary work(s) with bibliographic context (author, year, genre).
  - Statement of the critical angle: feminist, postcolonial, ecocritical, psychoanalytic, Marxist.
  - Identification of the interpretive gap in existing literary scholarship.
  - Thesis: central interpretive argument about the text.
  - Scope: specific aspects to be analyzed (narrative structure, characterization, imagery).

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Critical Context" / "Scholarly Background" / "Literature Review"
  - Survey of existing critical interpretations of the work/author.
  - Situate within relevant critical theory: Said's Orientalism, Spivak's subaltern,
    Butler's gender performativity, Freudian psychoanalysis, Marxist criticism.
  - Review genre studies relevant to the work (novel theory, lyric poetry, dramatic theory).
  - Engage with reception history: how the text was read across different periods.
  Key frameworks: New Criticism, Deconstruction, New Historicism, Ecocriticism,
  Postcolonialism, Queer Theory, Affect Theory.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Critical Approach" / "Methodology" / "Theoretical Framework"
  - Close reading: detailed analysis of specific passages, imagery, figurative language.
  - Narrative analysis: narrator, focalization, narrative time, plot structure.
  - Discourse analysis: ideological content, power relations in language.
  - Intertextual analysis: tracing allusions, influences, dialogism (Bakhtin).
  - Genre analysis: conventions, subversions, hybridization.
  - Thematic analysis: identifying recurring themes and their development.
  - Comparative literary analysis: cross-cultural or cross-linguistic comparison.
  Data: literary texts, author interviews, historical documents, critical essays.

RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  Section name: "Analysis" / "Reading" / thematic chapter titles
  - Textual evidence: direct quotations from the literary work with page citations.
  - Analysis of specific literary devices (metaphor, irony, symbolism, alliteration).
  - Argument built through sequential close reading of passages.
  - Connection between textual features and broader social/ideological meanings.
  Estimated: 3–6 passages analyzed in detail; 10–20 works of scholarship engaged.
  Tables: motif frequency, character appearance analysis, structural comparison.
  Figures: character relationship diagram, narrative arc chart.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Narrative structure diagram (Freytag's pyramid or narrative arc).
  - Character relationship map.
  - Thematic word cloud or frequency analysis.
  - Intertextual connection diagram.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Motif/symbol inventory (symbol | text location | interpretation).
  - Character analysis matrix (character | role | arc | symbolic function).
  - Comparative works analysis (work | period | critical approach | central theme).

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Restates interpretive thesis with synthesis of textual evidence.
  - Reflects on implications for understanding the author, period, or broader human condition.
  - Acknowledges interpretive limitations and alternative readings.
  - Suggests new avenues: applying different theory, comparative study, translation analysis.

ADDITIONAL SECTIONS:
  - "Note on the Text": edition used, translation issues, textual variants.
  - "Biographical Context": relevant author biography (if historicist approach).

DOMAIN VOCABULARY:
  diegesis, narrator, focalization, intertextuality, polyphony, dialogism, allegory,
  irony, mimesis, catharsis, trope, motif, archetype, bildungsroman, unreliable narrator,
  stream of consciousness, magical realism, gothic, subaltern

METHODOLOGY KEYWORDS:
  close reading, narrative analysis, discourse analysis, intertextual analysis,
  genre analysis, thematic analysis, postcolonial reading, feminist criticism,
  deconstruction, psychoanalytic criticism, ecocriticism
