FIELD: Engineering / Environmental Science
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: IEEE or APA
SUBDISCIPLINE: Solar PV, wind energy, biomass, geothermal, hydropower, hydrogen, energy storage, smart grids, hybrid renewable systems

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. State the domain problem, specific object or system, research gap, method, dataset/materials, validation approach, key quantitative results, and practical or scientific contribution. Include measurable variables and avoid broad claims unsupported by evidence.
  Domain emphasis: renewable resource assessment, conversion efficiency, grid integration, storage coordination, techno-economic feasibility, emissions reduction, reliability, and policy relevance.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Context and urgency of the domain problem.
  - Specific system, material, process, population, site, or dataset.
  - Research gap in current methods, standards, materials, models, or local operating conditions.
  - Novelty and contribution relative to recent peer-reviewed studies.
  - Objectives, scope, assumptions, limitations, and expected measurable outcomes.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Literature Review" / "Background" / "Related Work"
  - Define core concepts, mechanisms, theories, standards, and performance indicators.
  - Compare recent empirical, computational, design, and review studies.
  - Identify methodological limitations, inconsistent findings, and unresolved engineering or scientific issues.
  - Position the proposed work against benchmark methods, datasets, materials, or case studies.
  - End with a clear gap statement that motivates the study.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Materials and Methods" / "Methodology" / "Research Method"
  - Describe study design, variables, site/material/system selection, sample size, and boundary conditions.
  - Specify instruments, software, standards, experimental setup, computational model, survey protocol, or data source.
  - Define preprocessing, calibration, validation, sensitivity analysis, uncertainty handling, and quality control.
  - State evaluation metrics, statistical tests, equations, assumptions, and reproducibility details.
  - Include ethical, safety, environmental, or operational constraints when relevant.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Report descriptive results before comparative or inferential analysis.
  - Compare measured, simulated, and benchmark values using domain-specific metrics.
  - Explain mechanisms behind trends, anomalies, failures, trade-offs, and uncertainty.
  - Discuss implications for design, operation, policy, sustainability, cost, reliability, or scalability.
  - Connect findings to literature and state limitations affecting generalization.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Conceptual framework or system architecture diagram.
  - Study area, sample, material, device, or experimental setup image.
  - Workflow, model pipeline, or process flow diagram.
  - Performance comparison chart with uncertainty/error bars.
  - Spatial map, time-series profile, contour plot, spectrum, micrograph, or response curve as applicable.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Input variables, parameters, materials, samples, or system specifications.
  - Experimental, survey, simulation, or observational design matrix.
  - Benchmark comparison across methods, models, materials, sites, or scenarios.
  - Validation metrics, statistical results, uncertainty ranges, or sensitivity analysis.
  - Practical recommendations, design criteria, cost indicators, or implementation constraints.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Answer each objective with concise quantitative or evidence-based findings.
  - State the main contribution, practical significance, and conditions where results apply.
  - Mention limitations in data, scale, assumptions, site specificity, instruments, or model validity.
  - Recommend future work on field validation, long-term monitoring, larger datasets, optimization, or deployment.
