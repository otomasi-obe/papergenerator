TOPIC GUIDE — Religious Studies

FIELD: Arts and Humanities / Religious Studies
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: Chicago
SUBDISCIPLINE: Teologi, Studi Agama-Agama, Sejarah Agama, Sosiologi Agama

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. Elements: religion/tradition studied, analytical approach (theological, historical,
  sociological, comparative), key text/practice/institution analyzed, main argument.
  Example: "This paper examines how Sufi mystical practices in 13th-century Anatolia..."

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Introduction of the religious tradition, text, or practice studied.
  - Contextualize within the study of religion as an academic discipline.
  - Identify the scholarly debate or hermeneutical problem.
  - State the paper's approach: phenomenological, historical-critical, sociological, theological.
  - Research significance: interfaith dialogue, religious history, contemporary practice.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Scholarly Context" / "Literature Review" / "Theological Background"
  - Engage with foundational texts of the tradition under study (scripture, commentaries).
  - Review scholarship in history of religions: Eliade, Smith, Durkheim, Weber, Clifford Geertz.
  - For theology: patristic sources, scholastic tradition, contemporary systematic theology.
  - Comparative religion approaches: Huston Smith, Karen Armstrong, Wilfred Cantwell Smith.
  - Sociological studies of religion: secularization thesis, new religious movements.
  Key frameworks: Phenomenology of religion, Historical-critical method, Liberation theology,
  Mysticism studies, Comparative religion.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Method" / "Analytical Approach"
  - Textual exegesis: close reading of sacred texts, commentaries, theological documents.
  - Historical-critical method: source criticism, form criticism, redaction criticism.
  - Phenomenological analysis: bracketing presuppositions, describing religious experience.
  - Ethnographic fieldwork: participant observation of religious communities/rituals.
  - Sociological survey: religious affiliation, practice frequency, belief surveys.
  - Comparative method: structured comparison across traditions.
  - Hermeneutical analysis: interpretation of texts within community contexts.

TYPICAL RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  Section name: "Theological Analysis" / "Exegetical Findings" / "Discussion"
  - Exegetical findings: interpretation of specific scriptural passages.
  - Historical findings: development of doctrine, ritual, or institution over time.
  - Comparative findings: structural similarities/differences between traditions.
  - Sociological data: survey results on religious belief/practice patterns.
  Estimated: "comparative analysis of 5 major textual traditions",
  "survey of 350 practitioners in 3 communities".
  Tables: comparative doctrine summary, ritual element comparison.
  Figures: religious site maps, iconographic analysis, demographic charts of religious affiliation.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Map of religious spread/diffusion.
  - Diagram of religious hierarchy or institutional structure.
  - Timeline of doctrinal development.
  - Iconographic analysis image.
  - Bar chart of religious practice frequency.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Comparative doctrine table (religion A | religion B | key difference).
  - Timeline of religious reform movements.
  - Survey results table (practice | frequency | % respondents).

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Summarizes theological/historical/sociological findings.
  - Discusses implications for interreligious dialogue or religious practice.
  - Acknowledges hermeneutical limitations, community sensitivities.
  - Suggests future research: unexplored texts, traditions, or communities.

ADDITIONAL SECTIONS:
  - "Hermeneutical Considerations": methodology of text interpretation.
  - "Ethical Dimensions": researcher positionality in studying sacred traditions.
