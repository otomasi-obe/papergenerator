================================================================================
TOPIC GUIDE — Water Resources
================================================================================
FIELD: Civil Engineering / Environmental Science
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: APA or IEEE
SUBDISCIPLINE: Hydrology, hydraulics, flood risk, irrigation, groundwater, water quality, watershed management, urban drainage, climate adaptation

COMMON REFERENCE VENUES:
  Journal of Hydrology, Water Resources Research, Hydrological Processes, Water Research, Advances in Water Resources, Journal of Water Resources Planning and Management

--- PAPER GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS ---
When given a user's research idea, you MUST generate a COMPLETE academic paper following these requirements:

1. RESEARCH GAP IDENTIFICATION:
   - Identify 3-5 specific gaps in current literature related to the user's topic
   - Reference recent works (2020-2025) that establish what is known
   - Clearly articulate what remains UNKNOWN or UNRESOLVED
   - Frame the gap as a researchable question with measurable outcomes
   - Ensure the gap targets Q1/Q2 Scopus-indexed journals

2. NOVEL TOPIC REFINEMENT:
   - Refine the user's idea into a publishable research title
   - The title must be specific, include method and application domain
   - Target journals listed in COMMON REFERENCE VENUES below

3. LATEST METHODOLOGY (2022-2025):
   - Use cutting-edge methods appropriate to the subdiscipline
   - Include complete research procedures and protocols
   - Specify all tools, software, instruments, and configurations
   - Detail sample size calculations, validation strategy, and reproducibility measures

4. ESTIMATED DATA & RESULTS:
   - Provide realistic estimated data tables (10-30 data points)
   - Include statistical measures: mean, std, 95% CI, effect sizes
   - Performance/outcome metrics appropriate to the field
   - Comparison with control groups or baseline conditions
   - All numbers must be realistic, internally consistent, and domain-appropriate
   - Data must support the research conclusions logically

5. MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS:
   - Include 5-10 relevant formulas with full variable definitions
   - Statistical tests, measurement models, analytical equations
   - Connect formulas to the methodology and results

6. LITERATURE REVIEW WITH EXPLICIT GAPS:
   - Minimum 25-30 references organized thematically
   - Structure: (a) foundational theories, (b) recent advances 2020-2025, (c) limitations
   - Each subsection MUST end with: "However, no study has addressed..."
   - Use synthesis paragraphs, not citation lists
   - Include Indonesian/Southeast Asian context where relevant

7. COMPLETE PAPER STRUCTURE:
   - Title, Abstract (200-250 words), Keywords (5-7)
   - Introduction (1500-2000 words)
   - Literature Review (2000-3000 words)
   - Methodology (2000-3000 words with formulas)
   - Results & Discussion (2000-3000 words with tables and figure descriptions)
   - Conclusion (500-800 words)
   - References (30+ entries, 60%+ from 2020-2025)
--- END PAPER GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS ---

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  150–250 words. State the domain problem, specific object or system, research gap, method, dataset/materials, validation approach, key quantitative results, and practical or scientific contribution. Include measurable variables and avoid broad claims unsupported by evidence.
  Domain emphasis: water availability, hydrological processes, hydraulic performance, water quality, flood or drought risk, infrastructure planning, climate variability, and stakeholder impact.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Context and urgency of the domain problem.
  - Specific system, material, process, population, site, or dataset.
  - Research gap in current methods, standards, materials, models, or local operating conditions.
  - Novelty and contribution relative to recent peer-reviewed studies.
  - Objectives, scope, assumptions, limitations, and expected measurable outcomes.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Literature Review" / "Background" / "Related Work"
  - Define core concepts, mechanisms, theories, standards, and performance indicators.
  - Compare recent empirical, computational, design, and review studies.
  - Identify methodological limitations, inconsistent findings, and unresolved engineering or scientific issues.
  - Position the proposed work against benchmark methods, datasets, materials, or case studies.
  - End with a clear gap statement that motivates the study.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Materials and Methods" / "Methodology" / "Research Method"
  - Describe study design, variables, site/material/system selection, sample size, and boundary conditions.
  - Specify instruments, software, standards, experimental setup, computational model, survey protocol, or data source.
  - Define preprocessing, calibration, validation, sensitivity analysis, uncertainty handling, and quality control.
  - State evaluation metrics, statistical tests, equations, assumptions, and reproducibility details.
  - Include ethical, safety, environmental, or operational constraints when relevant.

RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Report descriptive results before comparative or inferential analysis.
  - Compare measured, simulated, and benchmark values using domain-specific metrics.
  - Explain mechanisms behind trends, anomalies, failures, trade-offs, and uncertainty.
  - Discuss implications for design, operation, policy, sustainability, cost, reliability, or scalability.
  - Connect findings to literature and state limitations affecting generalization.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Conceptual framework or system architecture diagram.
  - Study area, sample, material, device, or experimental setup image.
  - Workflow, model pipeline, or process flow diagram.
  - Performance comparison chart with uncertainty/error bars.
  - Spatial map, time-series profile, contour plot, spectrum, micrograph, or response curve as applicable.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Input variables, parameters, materials, samples, or system specifications.
  - Experimental, survey, simulation, or observational design matrix.
  - Benchmark comparison across methods, models, materials, sites, or scenarios.
  - Validation metrics, statistical results, uncertainty ranges, or sensitivity analysis.
  - Practical recommendations, design criteria, cost indicators, or implementation constraints.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Answer each objective with concise quantitative or evidence-based findings.
  - State the main contribution, practical significance, and conditions where results apply.
  - Mention limitations in data, scale, assumptions, site specificity, instruments, or model validity.
  - Recommend future work on field validation, long-term monitoring, larger datasets, optimization, or deployment.

DOMAIN VOCABULARY:
  watershed, runoff, hydrograph, discharge, return period, Manning coefficient, curve number, infiltration, evapotranspiration, aquifer, hydraulic conductivity, floodplain, sediment load, water quality index

METHODOLOGY KEYWORDS:
  HEC-HMS modeling, HEC-RAS modeling, SWAT modeling, MODFLOW groundwater simulation, rainfall-runoff analysis, frequency analysis, water quality sampling, flood inundation mapping, calibration and validation
