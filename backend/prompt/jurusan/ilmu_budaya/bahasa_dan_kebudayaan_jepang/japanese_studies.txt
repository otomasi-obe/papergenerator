  Nihongo Kyouiku, Journal of Japanese Linguistics, Japanese Language and Literature,
  Journal of Japanese Studies, Monumenta Nipponica, Japan Forum, Contemporary Japan,
  Asian Studies Review, JALT Journal, Language Learning, Studies in Second Language
  Acquisition, Jurnal Lingua Cultura, Japanology Journal Indonesia

--- PAPER GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS ---
When given a user's research idea, you MUST generate a COMPLETE academic paper following these requirements:

1. RESEARCH GAP IDENTIFICATION:
   - Identify 3-5 specific gaps in current literature
   - Reference recent works (2020-2025)
   - Clearly articulate what remains UNKNOWN or UNRESOLVED
   - Frame as researchable question with measurable outcomes
   - Target Q1/Q2 Scopus-indexed journals

2. NOVEL TOPIC REFINEMENT:
   - Refine into publishable research title (specific, with method and domain)

3. LATEST METHODOLOGY (2022-2025):
   - Contrastive analysis (Japanese-Indonesian linguistic systems)
   - Error analysis (morphological, syntactic, pragmatic errors)
   - Discourse analysis (spoken and written Japanese discourse)
   - Ethnography (Japanese cultural practices observation)
   - Cultural studies (popular culture, media analysis)
   - Translation studies (Venuti domestication/foreignization)
   - Corpus linguistics (Japanese language corpora, BCCWJ, CSJ)
   - SLA research (interlanguage, acquisition order, motivation)
   - Pragmatics (speech acts, politeness, indirectness)
   - Technology-enhanced learning (AI tutors, VR immersion, apps)

4. ESTIMATED DATA & RESULTS:
   - Realistic estimated data tables (10-30 data points)
   - Field-appropriate metrics (accuracy rates, error frequencies, proficiency scores, cultural adaptation indices)
   - All data realistic and internally consistent

5. MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS (where applicable):
   - Corpus linguistics: frequency, TF-IDF, collocational strength (MI score, t-score, log-likelihood)
   - Readability: Flesch-Kincaid, Gunning Fog
   - Inter-rater reliability: Cohen's kappa, Krippendorff's alpha
   - SLA metrics: accuracy/fluency/complexity measures, error rate calculations
   - Statistical tests: t-test, ANOVA, chi-square, effect size (Cohen's d)

6. LITERATURE REVIEW WITH EXPLICIT GAPS:
   - 25-30 references thematically organized
   - Each subsection ends with "However, no study has addressed..."
   - Indonesian/Asian context prioritized
   - Include Japanese studies scholars and JLPT/JF Standard references

7. COMPLETE PAPER STRUCTURE:
   Title, Abstract (200-250 words), Keywords, Introduction (1500-2000 words), Literature Review (2000-3000 words), Methodology (2000-3000 words), Results & Discussion (2000-3000 words), Conclusion (500-800 words), References (30+, 60% from 2020-2025)
--- END PAPER GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS ---

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  200-250 words. State the research problem, learner/cultural context, theoretical framework,
  methodology, principal findings, contribution to Japanese studies, limitation, and
  implication for pedagogy or cross-cultural understanding.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  - Define the linguistic or cultural phenomenon and its significance for Indonesian learners/scholars.
  - Situate within Japan-Indonesia relations and contemporary academic discourse.
  - Position against current scholarship in Japanese studies and applied linguistics.
  - State research gap, research questions, objectives, and expected contribution.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Common section name: "Kajian Pustaka" / "Literature Review" / "Theoretical Background"
  - Core theories in SLA, linguistics, cultural studies, or translation studies.
  - Recent empirical findings on Japanese language learning in Indonesian context.
  - JLPT proficiency framework and JF Standard for Japanese-Language Education.
  - Gaps in existing research on Indonesian learners of Japanese.

METHODOLOGY:
  Common section name: "Metode Penelitian" / "Research Methods"
  - Design: experimental, quasi-experimental, qualitative, mixed methods, or corpus-based.
  - Participants: Indonesian learners of Japanese (university level, JLPT level specification).
  - Instruments: tests, questionnaires, interviews, think-aloud protocols, corpus tools.
  - Analysis: statistical tests, error taxonomy, discourse coding, thematic analysis.

RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  - Present findings by research question with statistical or qualitative evidence.
  - Compare with prior studies on Japanese learners from other L1 backgrounds.
  - Discuss L1 transfer effects, pedagogical implications, and cultural factors.
  - Tables: error frequency, proficiency scores, survey results, translation comparison.
  - Figures: acquisition curves, error distribution charts, cultural model diagrams.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  - Research design flowchart.
  - Error type distribution chart.
  - Proficiency development trajectory.
  - Cultural dimension comparison radar chart.
  - Translation strategy distribution diagram.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  - Participant demographics: level | JLPT | years of study | L1 | institution.
  - Error analysis: error type | frequency | percentage | example | L1 influence.
  - Survey results: item | mean | SD | category | interpretation.
  - Translation comparison: source text | strategy A | strategy B | evaluation.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Answer research questions with specific data-supported claims.
  - State pedagogical and theoretical contributions.
  - Acknowledge limitations in sample size, context, and generalizability.
  - Recommend future research with specific populations, methods, or contexts.

DOMAIN VOCABULARY:
  Nihongo kyouiku, gengo shutoku, goyou bunseki, taishuu bunka, ibunka rikai,
  keigo, danwa bunseki, hon'yaku, tsuuyaku, nihongo nouryoku shiken, interlanguage,
  fossilization, pragmatic transfer, cultural adaptation, domestication, foreignization

METHODOLOGY KEYWORDS:
  contrastive analysis, error analysis, interlanguage analysis, discourse analysis, ethnographic observation, cultural studies methodology, translation comparison, corpus-based analysis, experimental design, quasi-experimental design, survey research, interview-based inquiry, think-aloud protocol, action research
