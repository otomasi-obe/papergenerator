TOPIC GUIDE — Food Science

FIELD: Natural Sciences / Food Technology
DEFAULT CITATION STYLE: APA or ACS
SUBDISCIPLINE:

COMMON REFERENCE VENUES:
  Food Chemistry, Journal of Food Science, LWT - Food Science and Technology, Food Quality and Preference, Innovative Food Science and Emerging Technologies, Trends in Food Science & Technology, Comprehensive Reviews in Food Science and Food Safety, Food Control, Food Research International, and Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry.

  Food chemistry, food microbiology, processing technology, packaging, sensory science, functional foods, rheology, sustainability, authentication, nutrition, public health reformulation, and food quality analytics.

--- PAPER GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS ---
When given a user's research idea, you MUST generate a COMPLETE academic paper following these requirements:

1. RESEARCH GAP IDENTIFICATION:
   - Identify 3-5 specific gaps in current literature related to the user's topic
   - Reference recent works (2020-2025) that establish what is known
   - Clearly articulate what remains UNKNOWN or UNRESOLVED
   - Frame the gap as a researchable question with measurable outcomes
   - Ensure the gap targets Q1/Q2 Scopus-indexed journals

2. NOVEL TOPIC REFINEMENT:
   - Refine the user's idea into a publishable research title
   - The title must be specific, include method and application domain
   - Target journals listed in COMMON REFERENCE VENUES below

3. LATEST METHODOLOGY (2022-2025):
   - Use cutting-edge methods appropriate to the subdiscipline
   - Include complete research procedures and protocols
   - Specify all tools, software, instruments, and configurations
   - Detail sample size calculations, validation strategy, and reproducibility measures

4. ESTIMATED DATA & RESULTS:
   - Provide realistic estimated data tables (10-30 data points)
   - Include statistical measures: mean, std, 95% CI, effect sizes
   - Performance/outcome metrics appropriate to the field
   - Comparison with control groups or baseline conditions
   - All numbers must be realistic, internally consistent, and domain-appropriate
   - Data must support the research conclusions logically

5. MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS:
   - Include 5-10 relevant formulas with full variable definitions
   - Statistical tests, measurement models, analytical equations
   - Connect formulas to the methodology and results

6. LITERATURE REVIEW WITH EXPLICIT GAPS:
   - Minimum 25-30 references organized thematically
   - Structure: (a) foundational theories, (b) recent advances 2020-2025, (c) limitations
   - Each subsection MUST end with: "However, no study has addressed..."
   - Use synthesis paragraphs, not citation lists
   - Include Indonesian/Southeast Asian context where relevant

7. COMPLETE PAPER STRUCTURE:
   - Title, Abstract (200-250 words), Keywords (5-7)
   - Introduction (1500-2000 words)
   - Literature Review (2000-3000 words)
   - Methodology (2000-3000 words with formulas)
   - Results & Discussion (2000-3000 words with tables and figure descriptions)
   - Conclusion (500-800 words)
   - References (30+ entries, 60%+ from 2020-2025)
--- END PAPER GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS ---

ABSTRACT CHARACTERISTICS:
  Structured or semi-structured abstracts should state the food material or system, research gap, objective, experimental design, analytical methods, primary quantitative outcomes, practical implication, and limitation in 150–250 words.

INTRODUCTION ELEMENTS:
  Establish food safety, quality, nutrition, sustainability, or consumer relevance; define the product or process context; identify measurable gaps in current evidence; justify variables, controls, and analytical approach; end with clear research objectives or hypotheses.

LITERATURE REVIEW:
  Compare recent peer-reviewed studies on food composition, processing, microbiology, sensory response, shelf life, packaging, functional ingredients, sustainability metrics, and analytical methods; synthesize disagreements, methodological limits, and opportunities for replication or optimization.

METHODOLOGY:
  proximate analysis, sensory evaluation, shelf-life study, food processing, microbiology testing, chromatography, rheology, nutritional analysis, response surface methodology, life-cycle assessment, randomized experimental design, ANOVA, PCA, PLS regression, kinetic modelling, and validation against standards.

RESULTS & DISCUSSION:
  Report composition changes, microbial counts, texture and rheological parameters, sensory scores, bioactive retention, shelf-life indicators, model performance, processing yield, environmental impacts, and trade-offs between safety, quality, cost, nutrition, and consumer acceptance.

TYPICAL FIGURES:
  sensory spider chart, texture profile curve, chromatogram, microbial growth curve, process flow diagram, shelf-life plot, PCA biplot, response surface plot, kinetic degradation curve, packaging schematic, and consumer preference map.

TYPICAL TABLES:
  proximate composition, sensory scores, microbial counts, nutritional facts, processing conditions, shelf-life parameters, formulation matrix, ANOVA summary, model performance metrics, quality standards comparison, and cost or life-cycle inventory.

CONCLUSION CHARACTERISTICS:
  Summarize the strongest evidence, quantify the main improvement or risk reduction, state the best-performing formulation or process condition, note practical constraints, and propose targeted validation, scale-up, or regulatory follow-up.

DOMAIN VOCABULARY:
  water activity, proximate composition, bioaccessibility, Maillard reaction, lipid oxidation, texture profile analysis, sensory acceptability, shelf life, HACCP, active packaging, edible coating, rheology, emulsion stability, antioxidant capacity, food matrix, clean label, nutraceutical, adulteration, traceability, and reformulation.

METHODOLOGY KEYWORDS:
  proximate analysis; GC-MS; LC-MS; HPLC; NIR spectroscopy; microbial enumeration; challenge test; shelf-life modelling; response surface methodology; central composite design; descriptive sensory analysis; hedonic test; CATA; rheometry; texture profile analysis; water activity measurement; accelerated storage; life-cycle assessment; chemometrics; PCA; PLS regression; ANOVA.
