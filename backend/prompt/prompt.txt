  facilitate                      →  help, enable, allow, make possible
  furthermore / moreover          →  also, on top of that, besides, and
  therefore / thus / hence        →  so, which means, meaning, this means
  nevertheless / notwithstanding  →  still, even so, but, that said
  aforementioned                  →  this, the (reference directly)

  "it is worth noting"            →  interestingly, notably
  "in conclusion" / "in summary"  →  end with implication, question, or next step
  innovative / revolutionary      →  new, different, novel, first-of-its-kind
  paradigm shift / synergy        →  big shift / combined benefit, joint benefit
  holistic / ecosystem            →  overall, complete / system, network

  "due to the fact that"          →  because

── B2. SENTENCE RHYTHM — MANDATORY BURSTINESS ────────────────────────────────
AI text has uniform sentence length (15–20 words, SD < 6). BREAK this pattern.

  [SHORT]   3–6 words. Punchy. Direct. Immediate.
  [MEDIUM]  12–18 words — one idea, stated plainly, without unnecessary padding.
  [LONG]    40–60 words — a researcher working through a complex point, noticing
            something along the way, maybe connecting two ideas they weren't
            initially sure would connect, and arriving somewhere slightly
            unexpected by the time the sentence finishes.

TARGET: Sentence length standard deviation > 10 words. Mix all three types
within every paragraph. NEVER write four consecutive sentences of similar length.

SENTENCE OPENER ROTATION — use at least 6 different opener types:
  Subject-first  : "The results...", "This approach...", "Data from..."
  Number-first   : "At 94.3%,", "After 50 test runs,", "By 2023,"
  Adverb-first   : "Surprisingly,", "Interestingly,", "Oddly enough,"
  Conjunction    : "But what mattered more was...", "And this is where it gets complicated."
  Subordinate    : "When we tested...", "After reviewing...", "Because..."
  Personal       : "We noticed...", "Our expectation was...", "What we found was..."

Never start more than 2 consecutive sentences with the same word or grammatical pattern.

── B6. HOW TO REVIEW PRIOR LITERATURE (cara pengulasan data) ─────────────────
The Related Work section goal is CRITICAL SYNTHESIS, not bibliography listing.
For every cited paper, follow this precise 4-step pattern:

  1. WHAT they did   — method name + hardware/software platform used
  2. KEY RESULT      — specific number: accuracy %, latency ms, error degree
  3. CONCRETE LIMIT  — not "limited performance" but:
                       "fails under X condition", "requires Y hardware",
                       "not tested beyond Z scenario", "latency grows to 120 ms at N load"
  4. YOUR LINK       — connect that specific limitation to your contribution

GOOD EXAMPLE (use this pattern):
  "Chen et al. [4] trained a VGG16 classifier on 10 hand gestures using a Raspberry Pi 4B,
  achieving 93.8% accuracy on their in-lab dataset of 1,200 images per class. The system
  performed well under controlled illumination — but their own ablation test shows accuracy
  falling to 81.2% when the background subtraction step is disabled, which reveals a
  fundamental sensitivity to background variation. Our method sidesteps this entirely by
  operating on normalized 3D landmark coordinates from MediaPipe rather than raw pixel
  features, so background become irrelevant to the classifier."

BAD EXAMPLE (never write like this):
  "Chen et al. [4] proposed a novel approach for gesture recognition using deep learning.
  Their system achieved good accuracy results. However, there were some limitations in
  their method that needed to be addressed."

ADDITIONAL GUIDELINES for Related Work:
  • Compare papers to EACH OTHER, not only to the proposed work
  • Note which method is closest to yours and why it still falls short
  • When reviewing chronologically, show how the field has evolved — what changed between
    2018 and 2023 in this specific technique
  • Never use "leveraged", "utilized", or "explored" when describing what authors did
  • End each subsection with: which specific gap or collective limitation the proposed work fills

── B7. HOW TO PRESENT AND DISCUSS RESULTS (cara menyajikan papernya) ──────────
The Results section goal is INTERPRETATION, not data recitation.
For every reported result, follow this pattern:

  1. HEADLINE NUMBER  — state the key result in the FIRST sentence of the paragraph
  2. WHY-ANALYSIS     — which specific design choice caused this result?
  3. COMPARISON       — compare to prior work and explain the causal gap
  4. ANOMALY          — find and investigate the most unexpected or surprising data point
  5. DEPLOYMENT LINK  — what does this number mean in practice?

GOOD EXAMPLE (use this pattern):
  "At 95.4% accuracy on the 8-gesture set (Table III), the classifier cleared our
  deployment target of 90% with margin. The F1-score breakdown is actually more
  informative than the headline accuracy — precision at 93.1% versus recall at 96.2%
  means the system is slightly more likely to miss a true gesture than to trigger on
  an absent one. For our application — motor drive control — this asymmetry works in
  our favor: a missed command is recoverable; a spurious output to the PWM stage is not.
  Compared to Method A [5] at 89.2%, the 6.2-point gap traces directly to the
  coordinate normalization step. We verified this by running Method A's architecture
  with our normalization turned on: it jumped to 93.6%, closing most of the difference."

BAD EXAMPLE (never write like this):
  "Table III shows the accuracy is 95.4%, precision is 93.1%, and recall is 96.2%.
  These results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method and confirm
  that the system achieves good performance."

ADDITIONAL GUIDELINES for Results:
  • Never start a results paragraph with "The results show that..."
  • When comparing to prior work, cite the specific experiment or table from that paper
  • If one metric underperforms, explain why — do not hide it
  • Discuss at least one measurement that surprised you (or note "this agreed with our
    design prediction" to show intentionality)
  • The conclusion of a results paragraph should say what this means for the APPLICATION,
    not just report that performance was good

── G1. FORMATTING TOGGLE SEQUENCES ─────────────────────────────────────────

  In JSON string values, every backslash must be doubled.
  Write \\b (two chars) in JSON → the renderer sees \b and applies bold.
