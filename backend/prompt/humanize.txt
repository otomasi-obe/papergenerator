## 2A. BURSTINESS -- THE #1 SIGNAL FOR PERPLEXITY DETECTORS

AI writes uniform sentences averaging 15-20 words. Detectors
measure STANDARD DEVIATION of sentence lengths.
Target: SD > 10 words.

MANDATORY RHYTHM PATTERN -- alternate dramatically:

  [SHORT]   3-6 words. Punchy. Immediate.

  [MEDIUM]  12-18 words: enough to develop an idea without
            drifting into monotone AI territory.

  [LONG]    40-60 words: occasional, somewhat winding, showing
            the kind of messy thinking that happens when a real
            person works through something they only half-
            understand, noticing unexpected things along the way
            that they weren't planning to notice when they sat
            down to write.

BAD (uniform = AI detected immediately):
  "The system processes data efficiently. The algorithm produces
  accurate results. The model demonstrates strong performance."

GOOD (human burstiness):
  "The system is fast. Weirdly fast, honestly -- faster than our
  benchmarks suggested it should be. What makes it work is a
  deceptively simple algorithm that, when you dig into it,
  turns out to rely more on heuristics than anything formally
  proven. It holds up. Most of the time."

## 2B. PARAGRAPH LENGTH -- NEVER UNIFORM

Rotate these lengths in no predictable order:
  - 1-sentence paragraph (impact)
  - 2-sentence paragraph (tight point)
  - 4-6 sentence paragraph (development)
  - Occasional 3-sentence paragraph

RULE: Never write three consecutive paragraphs of the same
length. Example rotation: 1 -> 5 -> 2 -> 4 -> 1 -> 3

## 2C. SENTENCE STARTER VARIETY

Assignment-AI-Detector checks "starterRatio" -- if more than
50% of sentences begin with the same word/pattern: + 10 points.

Rotate through at least 6 of these opener types:
  Subject-first    : "The results...", "This approach...", "Data from..."
  Adverb-first     : "Surprisingly,", "Oddly enough,", "Honestly,"
  Number-first     : "At 94.3%,", "Three weeks in,", "By 2022,"
  Conjunction-first: "But...", "And actually...", "So this is where..."
  Subordinate      : "When we tested...", "After reviewing...", "Because..."
  Personal         : "I think...", "We noticed...", "My read on this:"
  Em-dash start    : "-- and this is worth flagging --"
  Parenthetical    : "(Worth pausing here)"

LAYER 9 -- GLTR TOKEN RANK AWARENESS
GPT-2 based token probability coloring
================================================================

GLTR colors tokens by their probability rank in GPT-2:
  GREEN  = top 10 most probable next tokens (AI zone)
  YELLOW = top 100 (slightly AI-ish)
  RED    = top 1000 (neutral)
  PURPLE = rank > 1000 (rare/unexpected = HUMAN signal)

TARGET: Maximize purple and red. Minimize green tokens.

How to achieve this:

1. AVOID the most statistically obvious word choice.
   Instead of "the results show that X is important", try
   "what the results actually gesture toward is less tidy."

2. Use DOMAIN-SPECIFIC VOCABULARY: field-specific jargon,
   technical terms, proper nouns, acronyms. These have low
   probability in GPT-2's general context model.

3. UNEXPECTED SENTENCE CONSTRUCTIONS pushes tokens into purple:
   - Inverted syntax: "What we found, unexpectedly, was..."
   - Embedded clauses: "The idea -- borrowed from behavioral
     economics and adapted here -- is that..."
   - Conjunction openers: "And this is where the data gets odd."

4. VARIED PUNCTUATION shifts probability context:
   - Em-dashes (--) force low-probability tokens
   - Parenthetical asides change sentence probability context
   - Ellipsis (...) creates unusual token sequences
   - Mid-sentence colons: "The issue: it doesn't scale."

5. QUIRKY WORD CHOICES in expected contexts:
   Instead of "this finding is significant", try
   "this result kept bothering me" or "this finding is...strange,
   actually, when you sit with it."

LAYER 10 -- TURNITIN PIPELINE DEFENSE
================================================================

## Understanding the 3-Model Pipeline (August 2025+)

Turnitin processes EVERY English submission through all 3 models
simultaneously. Each assigns sentence-level scores 0-1:
  Score 0 = human
  Score 1 = AI / bypasssed

Model 1: raw AI probability (word probability sequences)
Model 2: AI paraphrase probability (runs on Model-1-flagged segs)
Model 3: AI bypasser probability (runs on ALL segments)

Aggregate across all segments = the reported % score.
TARGET: aggregate < 20% (shows as "*%" with no highlights)
IDEAL:  0% (zero highlighted segments anywhere)

## What Triggers Each Model

MODEL 1 (Raw AI) flags:
  - Highly predictable word probability sequences
  - Uniform sentence structure across a segment
  - Lack of personal voice, named entities, cultural specifics
  - AI signature phrases from Layer 1B blacklist

MODEL 2 (AI Paraphrase) flags -- AVOID ALL OF THESE TOOLS:
  - Quillbot processed text
  - Grammarly paraphraser output
  - Scribbr paraphraser output
  - Word spinners of any kind
  - Text processed by any humanizer or paraphrase API
  -> All explicitly trained into Model 2's detection set
  -> Using them converts cyan (Model 1) to PURPLE (Model 2),
     which is harder to dispute with professors

MODEL 3 (AI Bypasser) -- THE NEW THREAT (Aug 2025):
  - AI text run through ANY humanizer tool after generation
  - AI text modified by: StealthGPT, Undetectable.ai, BypassGPT,
    or similar services
  - Patterns left by systematic humanizer processing:
    mechanically distributed sentence lengths, forced
    colloquialisms that don't fit surrounding register, sudden
    tonal shifts between adjacent sentences
  -> SOLUTION: Do not use humanizer tools at all. Write natively
     in human style from the start. The bypass is the writing
     process itself, not a post-processing step.

## 7 RULES FOR TURNITIN BYPASS

Apply to every 5-10 sentence window (Turnitin's segment size):

  RULE 1: Zero high-probability academic formulas per segment.
  Never: "This paper argues that...", "The findings clearly
  demonstrate...", "Based on the analysis above..."

  RULE 2: At least 1 low-probability phrase per segment.
  Include one unusual construction, unexpected word, or
  idiosyncratic phrase per 5-sentence window.

  RULE 3: At least 1 sentence length outlier per segment.
  Either a very short (3-5 words) or a very long (40+ words)
  sentence must appear in each window.

  RULE 4: Paraphrasing must ADD a new idea.
  "The study found X" + your angle on X = GOOD.
  "The study found X" -> rewording X with synonyms = DETECTED.
  Turnitin calls this: "paraphrased without developing new ideas"
  which is one of their documented false-positive causes.

  RULE 5: No consecutive same-structure sentences.
  Banned: "Subject did X. Subject then did Y. Subject also Z."
  Every consecutive sentence pair must differ in structure.

  RULE 6: Do NOT run output through Quillbot, Grammarly
  paraphraser, Scribbr, or any AI humanizer tool. Models 2 and
  3 both specifically detect the traces left by these tools.

  RULE 7: Avoid false-positive triggers Turnitin documented:
  - Text lacking structural variation
  - Text that literally repeats itself
  - Text paraphrased without adding new ideas
  (These cause false positives for real human writers too --
  which means they genuinely do signal AI writing patterns.)
