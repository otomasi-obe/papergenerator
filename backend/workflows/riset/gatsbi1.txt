ai : ### Dynamic Shear-Pulse Compaction for Recycled 3D Printed Bridges
We introduce a reformulated material deposition system designed to overcome interlayer adhesion deficits inherent to recycled cementitious binders. The core innovation replaces conventional continuous extrusion with a **Dynamic Shear-Pulse Compaction (DSPC)** printhead module, which actively modulates extrusion pressure and nozzle kinematics to induce controlled particle migration and localized thixotropic recovery at each interface. This reformulation targets the **cementitious binder dry powder mixture**—comprising Portland cement and supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs) such as fly ash or slag—wherein a mass fraction \(f_r\) of the total binder is replaced by post-consumer recycled materials (e.g., ground recycled concrete powder, waste glass powder). The conventional gantry-based concrete printer is retained as the positioning platform, but its control architecture and material delivery subsystem are fundamentally altered.
#### Integration of the Dynamic Shear-Pulse Printhead
The existing progressive cavity pump delivers a homogeneous cementitious mortar (binder + water + admixtures) at a base volumetric flow rate \(Q_0\) into a novel **shear-pulse plenum** mounted immediately upstream of the print nozzle. This plenum contains a **piezoelectrically actuated diaphragm** that superimposes a high-frequency oscillatory pressure \(P_\text{osc}(t)\) onto the steady baseline pressure \(P_0\):
\[
P(t) = P_0 + P_\text{osc}(t) = P_0 + A_p \sin(2\pi f_p t)
\]
where \(A_p\) is the pressure amplitude (ranging from 0.5 to 5.0 bar, depending on material rheology) and \(f_p\) is the oscillation frequency (typically 20–100 Hz). This oscillatory component generates a periodic shear strain \(\gamma_\text{osc}(t)\) at the nozzle exit, which is superimposed on the mean extrusion shear rate \(\dot{\gamma}_0\). The net shear rate at the nozzle wall is:
\[
\dot{\gamma}(t) = \dot{\gamma}_0 + \dot{\gamma}_\text{osc}(t) = \dot{\gamma}_0 + \frac{A_p}{\eta(t) \cdot L} \sin(2\pi f_p t)
\]
where \(\eta(t)\) is the instantaneous apparent viscosity of the mortar (a function of thixotropic state) and \(L\) is the nozzle length. The printhead also incorporates a **capillary rheometry sensor** inline within the plenum—a slit die of width \(w\) and gap \(h\) with embedded pressure transducers—to measure the real-time shear stress \(\tau(t)\) and compute \(\eta(t)\) via:
\[
\eta(t) = \frac{h^3 w \Delta P_\text{slit}(t)}{12 Q(t) L_\text{slit}}
\]
where \(\Delta P_\text{slit}(t)\) is the pressure drop across the slit length \(L_\text{slit}\), and \(Q(t)\) is the instantaneous volumetric flow rate.
#### Closed-Loop Dwell-Time Modulation
A **feedback control algorithm** (implemented on a field-programmable gate array, FPGA) receives the measured viscosity \(\eta(t)\) and computes the **dimensionless thixotropic recovery index** \(\Theta_r(t)\):
\[
\Theta_r(t) = \frac{\eta(t) - \eta_\infty}{\eta_0 - \eta_\infty}
\]
where \(\eta_0\) is the initial (fully structured) viscosity and \(\eta_\infty\) is the equilibrium (fully sheared) viscosity at the applied shear rate. When \(\Theta_r(t)\) exceeds a threshold \(\Theta_\text{crit}\) (empirically determined as 0.65–0.85 for recycled binder blends), the controller activates a **dwell phase** by commanding the gantry to halt horizontal movement for a duration \(\Delta t_\text{dwell}\). During this dwell, the oscillation amplitude \(A_p\) is momentarily increased to a compaction pulse \(A_c\) (1.5–3.0 times \(A_p\)) for a short burst of 50–200 ms. This pulse forces fine recycled particulates (typical median diameter \(d_{50} = 5\)–\(15\ \mu\)m) into the micro-voids of the previously deposited layer via **shear-induced particle migration**, driven by the gradient in shear rate across the interlayer gap. The migration velocity \(v_m\) of a particle of radius \(r_p\) is approximated by:
\[
v_m = \frac{2 r_p^2 (\rho_p - \rho_f)}{9 \eta} \cdot \frac{d \dot{\gamma}}{dz}
\]
where \(\rho_p\) is the particle density, \(\rho_f\) is the fluid density, and \(d\dot{\gamma}/dz\) is the shear rate gradient normal to the layer interface (established by the oscillatory flow field). This controlled compaction simultaneously breaks down the local yield stress at the interface and triggers rapid structural rebuild via the thixotropic recovery mechanism, effectively forming a **mechanically interlocked transition zone** of depth \(\delta_z \approx 0.5\)–\(2.0\) mm.
#### Substitution of Conventional Inputs and Outputs
Conventionally, the gantry control system reads a G-code program specifying constant extrusion rates and constant print speeds. In the reformulated system, the **conventional G-code inputs** (desired layer height \(h_l\), nominal bead width \(w_b\), and print speed \(v_p\)) are retained but fed into a **gain scheduler** that translates them into base parameters for the DSPC module:
- The mean extrusion flow rate \(Q_0\) is set by \(Q_0 = h_l w_b v_p\).
- The nominal oscillation frequency \(f_p\) is set to \(f_p = \alpha / t_\text{layer}\) (where \(t_\text{layer}\) is the time to print one layer and \(\alpha\) is a scaling factor, typically 0.5–2.0).
The **output of the DSPC module** is a modified extrusion rate \(Q_\text{eff}(t)\) and a corrected gantry speed command \(v_\text{eff}(t)\):
\[
Q_\text{eff}(t) = Q_0 + \Delta Q_\text{pulse}(t), \quad \text{where } \Delta Q_\text{pulse}(t) = \frac{A_c}{P_0} Q_0 \text{ for } t \in [t_\text{dwell,start}, t_\text{dwell,end}]
\]
\[
v_\text{eff}(t) = 
\begin{cases} 
0, & \text{if dwell phase active} \\
v_p, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\]
These outputs replace the conventional constant commands to the pump and gantry drivers. The conventional pump's pressure regulator still operates on the baseline \(P_0\), but the DSPC module superimposes the oscillatory and pulse components via the piezoelectric actuator.
#### Concrete Realization of the Rheometry Sensor
The inline capillary rheometry sensor is realized as a **miniaturized slit-die rheometer** machined from hardened stainless steel, with slit dimensions \(w = 5\) mm, \(h = 1\) mm, and \(L_\text{slit} = 30\) mm. Two **flush-mounted piezoelectric pressure transducers** (Kistler 601CAA, resonant frequency > 100 kHz) are positioned 20 mm apart along the slit length, sampling at 2 kHz. The FPGA implements a **finite-impulse-response (FIR) moving average filter** (order 20, cutoff frequency 500 Hz) to reduce sensor noise before computing \(\eta(t)\). The threshold \(\Theta_\text{crit}\) is pre-calibrated for each recycled binder blend using a separate **oscillatory shear rheometer** (Anton Paar MCR 702, 25 mm parallel plate, 1 mm gap) to measure the full thixotropic loop—the evolution of \(\eta\) under the intended pulse shear history—and to identify the \(\Theta_r\) value at which the interlayer tensile bond strength exceeds 60% of the bulk material strength.
#### Interaction with the Cementitious Binder Formulation
The recycled binder dry powder mixture is pre-blended in a planetary mixer (Eirich R02) with a water-to-binder ratio \(w/b = 0.32\)–\(0.38\) and a superplasticizer (polycarboxylate ether, 1.2% by mass of binder). The recycled material replacement fraction \(f_r\) is limited to 0.30 (i.e., 30% by mass) to maintain a yield stress \(\tau_y > 800\) Pa and a plastic viscosity \(\eta_p < 15\) Pa·s, as measured by a coaxial cylinder rheometer prior to printing. The DSPC system's ability to locally increase shear and compaction compensates for the higher water absorption and irregular particle morphology of recycled fines, which otherwise cause premature structuration and poor interlayer fusion.
